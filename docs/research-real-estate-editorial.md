# Editorial real estate website references

Research date: 2026-11-03. Ten live agency candidates for a larger visual shortlist, selected for architecture, editorial content, and distinctive positioning. All ten candidates received a rendered desktop homepage inspection. Mobile behavior, interaction and accessibility were not tested. Suggestions are transferable ideas, not claims about unobserved styling.

| Candidate and market | Type | Evidence and design direction to explore | Adaptation tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [The Modern House](https://themodernhouse.com/) — UK | Architecture-led estate agency | Pairs featured homes and past sales with a journal and a clear appraisal action. Explore a magazine-like home story followed by a simple seller pathway. | Its extensive listing and editorial library could overwhelm an individual agent's starter; support an empty state. |
| [Inigo](https://www.inigo.com/) — UK | Historic-home estate agency; companion to The Modern House | Organizes properties into Prime, New and Featured and interleaves its Almanac stories. Explore an architectural history story alongside a small set of featured homes. | A heritage direction is distinctive but may not suit all markets or architectural inventory. |
| [Aucoot](https://aucoot.com/) — UK | Estate agency | Navigation includes Journal, Design Directory and Story of a Sale, alongside Buy and Sell. Explore a sale case study and local creative directory as editorial modules. | Directory and case-study content require maintenance and owner permission. |
| [Hemingway+K](https://hemingwayandk.com/) — UK | Architecture and design-led estate agency | Connects a succinct design-led positioning statement to property browsing and a journal. Explore a strong thesis followed by curated feature cards and a short agent introduction. | Its curated property photography is central; avoid depending on fabricated listings or unlicensed imagery. |
| [Domus Nova](https://domusnova.com/) — London and Ibiza | Independent estate agency | Separates London and Ibiza paths, property and editorial content, and offers an appraisal. Explore a location selector and architecture-led stories integrated with services. | Multi-region and luxury positioning may be too elaborate for an individual agent; keep location choices optional. |

| [Lum & Co](https://www.lumandco.nz/) — Auckland, New Zealand | Boutique agency | Its home page positions the firm as boutique and explains property marketing and coordinating a sale with a purchase. Explore a concise, service-led narrative for a small team. | This is a content and positioning candidate pending visual review; premium photography and proof points need owner-supplied material. |
| [WHITEFOX New Zealand](https://whitefoxrealestate.co.nz/) — New Zealand | Agency network | Establishes New Zealand locations and presents a defined marketing approach. Explore a crisp location index and campaign process for sellers. | Its broader network and prestige positioning may not translate to every independent agent. |
| [Puente Romano Real Estate](https://www.puenteromanorealestate.com/) — Marbella, Spain | Resort estate agency operated by Panorama | A sunset and mountain film fills the viewport, framed by a signature-style masthead and thin pill-outline navigation. Local guides and a buyer’s guide support the resort-oriented presentation. | Resort and luxury presentation relies on substantial owner-supplied media and may not suit every market. |

## Rendered desktop observations

- **The Modern House:** white masthead and split hero with a large image to the left and an editorial serif headline to the right.
- **Aucoot:** white masthead with circular search and menu controls; full-bleed interiors photograph with an italic serif listing title and restrained pill outline button. A cookie banner covers the bottom of the viewport.
- **Hemingway+K:** architectural photograph fills the viewport; an exceptionally large sans-serif wordmark anchors the left, with a smaller serif positioning statement at right. A cookie notice overlays the lower right.
- **Domus Nova:** generous white gallery carousel centers a large image, with adjacent slides peeking into view.
- **Fantastic Frank:** full-screen architecture and a gigantic bold sans-serif wordmark at the bottom, paired with minimalist navigation; a strong visual standout.
- **Adrian William:** a serif line overlays a colorful art and lifestyle film.
- **Lum & Co:** charcoal and terracotta palette with a large lifestyle video; more conventional overall.
- **WHITEFOX New Zealand:** landscape film with sparse white navigation.
- **Puente Romano:** full-viewport sunset and mountain film, handwritten signature-style centered masthead, thin outlined Menu and Properties pills, and a down chevron create a polished luxury-resort feel.
- **Inigo:** split hero combines a large interior image with a muted green panel, inset architectural image and white serif copy; a large italic headline crosses the dividing line.

## Recommended first three to inspect visually

1. **Fantastic Frank** — its giant wordmark and immersive architectural photography make a bold, immediately distinctive direction.
2. **Hemingway+K** — oversized typography, architecture imagery, and a concise positioning statement translate to a focused independent-agent homepage.
3. **Inigo** — the overlapping serif headline, rich green panel, and paired imagery give historic-home storytelling a contrasting visual direction.

These are directional recommendations based on desktop views, not a full usability evaluation. The Modern House and Inigo are related brands, so avoid counting them as wholly independent design precedents when narrowing. These sources are inspiration only: do not copy their identity, text, layouts, photographs, listings, testimonials, or claims. Check each candidate's rendered desktop and mobile pages and interaction behavior before deciding which directions merit implementation.
